R&D at BarretOps

Printed impact protection, built on data.

BarretOps does applied research in 3D-printed energy-absorbing structures. Our current project is a TPU lattice helmet liner for ice hockey, starting with youth players.

The problem

Most hockey helmets still use foam liners with one density throughout, tested at a single high impact speed. Young players take lower-speed hits more often, and those liners aren’t tuned for them.

What we’re researching

  • Printed TPU gyroid lattices, where geometry, not just material, controls how impacts are absorbed
  • Lattice density tuned to the impact energies of each level of play, from youth to adult rec to pro
  • Repeatable printing on accessible desktop equipment

How we work

  • One variable changed per test round
  • Every print and test logged with a run sheet and records
  • Data-driven decisions, with no claims we can’t back up

Where we are

Early stage. We’re tuning lattice print reliability on test coupons now. Impact testing comes next, and then helmet-level comparisons against stock foam liners.

Certification

Our prototypes are for research only and are not for use in games. Any production liner would go through testing to ASTM F1045 with a helmet manufacturer, the established path for hockey helmet certification.

Founder

Barret Wilde grew up in agricultural research as an assistant on GLP field trials, then went on to unmanned aircraft systems and production 3D printing. He applies the same research discipline here.
